# Customer guide

ResolveDesk is where you see your orders and get help when something goes wrong with one. You
tell us what happened, we check the order's records, and a person approves any refund before it
is paid.

## Signing in

1. Open the app. You see the **Sign in** page.
2. Under **Business**, choose the restaurant or cafÃ© you ordered from.
3. Enter the **email** and **password** for your account, then click **Sign in**.

If you see **"Wrong email or password"**, check both and try again. After five wrong tries the
app says **"Too many attempts, try again in a few minutes"**. Wait about 15 minutes before trying
again.

You stay signed in while this browser tab is open. If you are away for a long time, or close the
tab, you will see **"Your session has ended"**. Just sign in again. Click **Sign out** (top right)
when you are done on a shared computer.

## My orders

**My orders** lists your recent orders as cards. Each card shows:

- the **order number** (for example `O0001234`),
- the **brand and outlet** that made it,
- the **date and time** you placed it,
- the **total** in rupees,
- the order's **status** (for example *Delivered*).

Click a card to open the order. The order page shows:

- every **item**, with how many you **ordered** and how many the records say you **received**. A
  received number in red means fewer arrived than you ordered;
- the time the order was **promised by** and the time it was **delivered**.

## Reporting a problem

1. Open the order from **My orders**.
2. Click **Report a problem**.
3. Under **What went wrong?**, pick the closest match, such as *Late delivery* or *Missing items*.
   If none fits, or you are not sure, pick **Not sure â€” just describe it** (when shown) and our
   assistant will work out the category from your message.
4. For some problems (for example a wrong item) a **How would you like this settled?** list appears,
   such as *Voucher* or *Ask for a refund*. Each choice shows what you would get, worked out for your order, and
   how long it usually takes: "Usually instant" or "After a team member approves it". Pick one. If only
   one remedy is possible, no list is shown. Your choice never changes the limits: the amount is always
   set by our rules, and a refund is a request that a team member decides on (the voucher is the default; the refund choice is not shown on orders you placed through a delivery app such as Swiggy or Zomato, because that app refunds those). If the option you picked
   cannot be given any more, we use another that is allowed and note it. If you did not pick a category yet
   (*Not sure*), the same list may appear on the case page while we are still looking at your report.
5. Under **Tell us what happened**, describe the problem in your own words.
6. Click **Submit report**.

You go straight to the **case page** for your report.

**Privacy requests.** To ask about your personal data, pick **Privacy request** as the category. It is
never handled by the assistant: it goes straight to a member of our team, and the case page names our
**Privacy and grievance contact** and the date by which we aim to respond. That date is our own internal target, not a
legal deadline. We confirm we have your message within 48 hours. The Privacy and grievance contact is also linked in the footer of every page and on the **Help** page.

## My cases and the case page

**My cases** lists every problem you have reported, newest first, with its current stage. Click
one to open it.

The case page shows:

- a **progress bar** (the stages below), with the current stage highlighted;
- a **message** telling you where things stand right now;
- the **proposed remedy**, if there is one: a refund or a voucher, with the amount;
- a **timeline** of what has happened so far, with times.

While the case is *Investigating* or *Processing*, the page updates by itself every few seconds.
If it takes more than two minutes, click **Refresh** to check again.

If the case is **Awaiting info**, we have asked you a question and a **Your reply** box appears on
the case page. Type your answer and press **Send reply**; the case carries on by itself and the
timeline shows "You replied".

### What each stage means

| Stage | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Received** | We have your report. |
| **Investigating** | We are checking your order's records: items, times and payment. |
| **Waiting for your reply** | We need one more detail from you. The message says what. |
| **Awaiting approval** | We have proposed a remedy and a member of staff is reviewing it. |
| **Processing** | It was approved (by staff, or automatically for a small voucher) and is being carried out, for example the refund has been sent to the payment provider. |
| **Confirmed** | Done. For a refund, the payment provider has confirmed it; for a voucher, it has been issued. |
| **Declined** | Staff reviewed it and no remedy was approved. The message explains. |
| **With our team** | A person is handling your case directly. |
| **Closed** | The case is finished. If we closed it without a refund or voucher, the message says so in plain words, how to write to our privacy and grievance contact if you think we missed something (we reply within 2 working days and aim to settle within 30 days), and that you can call the National Consumer Helpline on 1915 (consumerhelpline.gov.in). |

## What happens after you report

1. Our assistant reads your message and checks the facts on record for **your** order only.
2. It looks up your business's policy and proposes a remedy (for example a refund for a missing
   item, or a voucher). The amount comes from the business's rules, not from the assistant.
3. **A person approves every refund.** Some small vouchers may be approved automatically.
4. When a refund is approved, it is sent to the payment provider. Your case says **Confirmed**
   only after the provider confirms the money has moved.

## What the assistant does and doesn't do

- It **does** check your order's real records and propose a fair remedy under the policy.
- It **does** ask you a short question if something is unclear.
- It **does not** pay anything itself. People approve refunds.
- It **never promises money** before the payment provider confirms it. If the case says
  *Processing*, the refund is on its way but not yet confirmed.
- It only ever looks at **your** orders.

## Our policies

Open **Our policies** in the menu to read the rules we follow. Each policy shows the date it came into force, and each section has its own link, so you can share or bookmark the exact part you mean. The text is shown exactly as written. You only see your own business's policies.

Above the policies, **Rules in plain words** says what you get for each kind of problem, for example a voucher up to the value of your order and at most a stated amount, or a refund of up to a share of your order after a team member approves it. The figures come straight from your business's current rules, so they change when the rules change, and the date they have been in force is shown at the top. It also says when a team member decides instead of the system.

On a report's page, the remedy card says in one plain line why the amount is what it is, and links to the exact policy section it is based on. If that section has since changed, the link opens the policy and says so.

## Privacy and data

Open **Privacy & data** in the menu.

- **What we hold about you.** A table at the top lists each kind of data we hold (your name,
  masked phone number, orders, support messages, payment references, refund and voucher records
  and your offer choices), your own count for each, why we keep it, how long, and whether it is
  used to help with orders or for offers. Message text may be removed some time after a case
  closes; your name and contact details are kept while you have an account, until you ask us to
  erase them. The retention periods are marked **provisional** until the business confirms them. Below the table, **Who else sees it** names the companies that
  process data for us. A **What we do not keep** line appears only when the business has
  confirmed the claim (today: we never see or store your card number, CVV or UPI PIN). The **Privacy and grievance contact** (name, email, and our own targets: we confirm we have your message within 48 hours and aim to reply within 30 days, not a legal deadline;
  "Not set yet" until the business names someone) shows at the top and bottom of the page. The page uses your chosen
  language.
- **Offers and promotions.** Each offer choice explains what you get (for example offers and vouchers for your next order), what we send, how often (at most one offer message in 30 days) and on which channel (WhatsApp). The switch starts **Off**, the label and the word **On** or **Off** are always visible, and nothing is ticked for you. To stop offers, turn the switch off: one click, and it applies to the next message we would send. **Help messages** are shown as always on, because they are needed to resolve your order; they have no switch, and turning offers off never changes them. **History of your choices** lists each change with its date, channel and the wording version in force. The page uses your chosen language.
- **Download my data.** Saves a file with your details, orders, cases, your choices above, and the
  companies that process data for us (such as the AI providers and the database host). The file is
  for other software. For a copy you can read, click **Open readable version**: one page in your
  language with a section for your details, orders, help requests, refunds and vouchers, your
  choices about offers, and the companies that handle data for us. Each section says why we hold
  it and how long we keep it. Amounts are in rupees and dates show the month name, in Indian time.
  Click **Print or save as PDF** to keep a copy. It shows only your own data, nothing internal.
- **Erase my data.** The card first lists **We will remove** (your name, phone and email, the text of
  your support messages, extra details on your profile, your sign-in, and your offers choice),
  **We will keep** with a reason for each (orders, payments and refunds for the record period,
  case records, a log of actions without your name, your record of offer choices, your customer
  reference code) and **We may wait** (a person approves first; an open case; a refund or
  payment still being processed; a record on hold; one year since your last order or case).
  Click **Request erasure**, read the confirmation (**Cancel** is selected first, and nothing is
  sent until you press **Yes, request erasure**). Then the card shows your request: **Received**,
  **Could not complete yet** with the reason, **Done**, or **Withdrawn**, the date we received
  it, the date we aim to finish by (the business's own target, 30 days unless it set another; we do not state a legal deadline) and
  the privacy and grievance contact. It says **Done** only after the erasure has
  really been carried out. A request that could not complete is not dropped: it stays open and a
  person re-checks it. Until it is carried out, press **Withdraw my request** to take it back;
  you can ask again later. Asking again while a request is open shows that same request. After
  it is done you can no longer sign in, because your sign-in is one of the things removed.
  **One message when it is done.** In the confirmation you choose where we tell you (your phone
  or your email, if we hold them). We keep that contact for up to 7 days, only to send this one
  message, then delete it. After the erasure has really been carried out we send it once: what
  we removed, what we kept and why (payment and refund records are kept up to 8 years, counted
  from the end of the financial year, and are marked provisional until the business confirms
  them), the privacy and grievance contact, and that you can complain to the Data Protection
  Board of India if you are still not satisfied after using our process. It never says "done"
  before the erasure has succeeded. If we hold no phone or email, the confirmation says so.

  an emoji, never change it.

  processed*, *done*, *failed* and *needs a check* clearly different. If a sentence has no
  translation, you see it in English.
