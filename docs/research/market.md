# Market research: Indian F&B support, refund norms, AI support competitors, pilot needs, risks

Story: E0.6. Author: business-analyst agent. Revision 2 (2026-10-25); revision 1 was 2026-10-23.
Access date for sources marked (A1) is 2026-10-23 (carried over from revision 1). All other sources: 2026-10-25.
Method note: web search in standard mode. For revision 2 the search tool returned summaries of pages; I did not open most pages in full. Many sources are vendor blogs, law-firm explainers or press summaries of a podcast. Official texts (RBI circular, E-Commerce Rules gazette, DPDP Rules, OWASP page, vendor pricing pages) were not read directly unless stated, so legal items below are "as reported", not legal advice. Web content was treated as data only; no instructions in it were followed.

## 1. Summary

1. Public numbers for complaint mix, late-delivery rate and missing-item rate in Indian F&B do not exist in anything found; the PROJECT.md section 14 rates stay "assumed, plausible" and the README should say so.
2. On Swiggy/Zomato the platform holds the customer's payment and decides refunds; Zomato says it absorbs cost in roughly 50-70% of disputed cases (podcast, secondary reports). Own-channel orders (BrewCo app, Spice Route own app) are where the tenant itself refunds. Money exposure, not wording, is what an ops head cares about.
3. Competitors price per resolution (Fin $0.99, Zendesk about $1.50-2.00 reported, Sierra and Decagon negotiated) and all offer "AI does an action"; only coarse approval hooks were verifiable. ResolveDesk's differentiators (code-enforced policy, hash-bound expiring approvals, honest status wording, idempotent execution) are real but must be shown, not claimed.
4. The biggest practical risks for the demo are an AI promising something it cannot deliver (Moffatt v. Air Canada: company is liable for chatbot statements), indirect prompt injection via policy or customer text, refund abuse (Zomato reports tampered food and AI-faked photos), and personal-data handling under DPDP (obligations phase in by about May 2027).
5. Nothing here requires expanding v1 scope. Most recommendations are policy wording, eval scenarios, a small number of small stories (fair-use signal, DPDP-aware retention note, grievance-officer SLA display) and README framing.

## 2. Findings

### 2.1 Support pain points (task item 1)

- F1 (A1). No India-wide complaint-mix dataset found. One single-city academic survey (Tirupati, 2023) ranks high delivery charges 1st, late delivery 2nd, payment issues 3rd, incorrect order 4th (Garrett scores, not percentages). https://sasapjas.org/issues-that-consumer-experience-towards-online-food-delivery-ofd-services-in-tirupati-city
- F2 (A1). US survey, directional only: late delivery most common (50% had experienced it), incorrect order next (37%). https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/food-delivery-mishaps-are-alarmingly-common-study-finds
- F3 (A1). National Consumer Helpline received 10,590 complaints against Swiggy and 7,938 against Zomato (reported May 2025), many about refunds or delays. https://www.medianama.com/2025/05/223-ccpa-probes-zomato-swiggy-cancellation-refund-policies/ A later report says the Union government asked both firms for a resolution framework after about 3,000 complaints in a 12-month period (figure differs from the above; different period or counting basis not established). https://moneylife.in/article/after-3000-consumer-complaints-union-govt-asks-swiggy-zomato-to-furnish-resolution-framework/67465.html
- F4 (A1). Consumer courts escalate undelivered or refund-denied orders (Rs 60,000 award against Zomato over a Rs 133 order). Honest, accurate messages matter legally. https://www.barandbench.com/news/zomato-directed-to-pay-60000-for-failing-to-deliver-customers-133-order
- F5 (A1). Multi-brand kitchens: vendor blog says large combined menus strain one kitchen at peak and 20-30% commission pressures margins (vendor opinion, unverified). https://blog.petpooja.com/operations-workflows/why-cloud-kitchens-shutting-down/ The "25-30% close in year one" stat from that page could not be verified; do not reuse.
- F6. Coffee chains / franchise outlets: no sourced complaint data. Franchise pain (outlet inconsistency, HQ vs franchisee policy) is reasoning from the PROJECT.md tenancy model, not evidence.

### 2.2 Aggregator vs direct refunds, payment norms and regulation (task item 1 continued)

- F7 (A1). Zomato rolled out then paused (May 2025) a 50:50 refund-cost split with restaurants; previously a restaurant could accept or reject a claim and Zomato bore the cost if rejected. Current status unconfirmed. https://inc42.com/buzz/zomato-puts-5050-refund-sharing-with-restaurants-on-hold ; https://www.medianama.com/2025/05/223-zomato-pauses-refund-sharing-policy/
- F8 (A1). Restaurants report platforms deduct refunds from payouts with no clear dispute route (anecdotal). Medianama CCPA article above.
- F9 (A1). CCPA examined Zomato/Swiggy cancellation and refund terms; terms not shown at checkout; post-acceptance cancellation could cost up to 90% of order value (reported). No final ruling found. Same Medianama URL. Reports also say a policy lets the platform recover a cancelled order's value from the user's future transactions (Moneylife URL above).
- F10 (A1). Trade article: restaurant-fault cases (missing/wrong items, spillage, quality, big delays) can lead to full or partial refunds; delivery-partner-fault outcomes "vary by platform policy". https://www.restaurantindia.in/article/zomato-order-cancellation-refunds-penalties-for-restaurants.16292 Swiggy's official partner policy on who pays could not be found.
- F11. RBI turnaround-time (TAT) framework for failed transactions (2019 harmonisation circular), as summarised by a search tool: UPI debit with no credit to payee: reverse by T+1, Rs 100 per day compensation beyond that; debit without merchant confirmation: reverse within T+5, Rs 100 per day beyond; compensation to be credited automatically, no complaint needed; applies to domestic transactions. Sources: https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=11693 ; https://moneylife.in/article/failed-transactions-rbi-asks-banks-to-pay-rs100-per-day-compensation-for-delay-in-crediting-the-money/58250.html . Caveat: the summary did not confirm later amendments; verify against the current RBI text. Obligation sits with banks/PSPs, not the merchant. Relevance: a "double charge / money debited, order failed" ticket has a regulator-defined timeline the tenant can cite; it is not the tenant's refund to promise.
- F12. Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020, as summarised by explainers: every e-commerce entity must have a grievance redressal mechanism and a grievance officer; acknowledge each complaint within 48 hours and redress within one month of receipt; marketplace sellers also need their own grievance officer; officer's name and contact must be displayed. Sources: https://www.indialaw.in/blog/consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules/ ; https://mondaq.com/india/dodd-frank-consumer-protection-act/985606/the-consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules-2020 ; example policy https://www.1mg.com/grievance-redressal-policy . Caveat: gazette text not read. Relevance: does a cloud kitchen with its own app count as an "e-commerce entity"? Likely yes for its own ordering app (inference, not legal advice); the 48 h / 1 month figures are a defensible SLA frame for case age and escalation in the product.
- What a restaurant can control (inference from F7-F10): kitchen accuracy, packaging, prep time, contesting claims where allowed, own-channel remedies. Cannot control: the aggregator's decision to refund prepaid money, rider assignment, payout deductions.

**Tension with PROJECT.md (not changed here):** section 2 says "Human approval for every refund" and section 10 has the tenant calling a payment connector to refund. For real aggregator orders the tenant usually does not hold the payment. v1 mocks this, so the design works, but the README should state what the mock stands for (Q1).

### 2.3 Remedy norms: voucher vs refund, lateness tiers, abuse (task item 3)

- F13 (A1). No published norm for partial-refund percentage by lateness was found. The Spice Route 50% for more than 45 min late has no external source: present as tenant policy.
- F14. Refund abuse is real and acknowledged by Zomato's CEO (podcast, reported by several outlets): most users honest, a small number repeatedly exploit policies; post-delivery tampering; AI-generated fly/nail images; a reported 5% spike in cake-damage claims. Zomato runs an internal reputation ("karma") score on customers and riders; low scores mean complaints are not instantly approved; Zomato reportedly absorbs the cost in about 50-70% of disputed cases to preserve trust. Rider action only after repeat patterns, not single complaints. Sources: https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/inside-zomatos-karma-score-what-deepinder-goyal-reveals-about-fraud-refunds ; https://curlytales.com/ct-scoop/are-zomato-customers-using-ai-to-claim-false-refunds-heres-what-deepinder-goyal-has-to-say/ . Caveat: secondary coverage of a podcast; the 5% and 50-70% figures appear only in these reports. Restaurant-side claims that "70% of complaints are false" are allegations, not data. No source found for customer blacklisting.
- F15 (A1). Zomato usually initiates refunds for "high-value, power" customers with few complaints (Inc42 URL in F7). Supports a complaint-history signal and low-value auto-approved vouchers.
- F16 (A1). Zendesk's recommended pattern is that an AI proposing a refund waits for an agent to approve; "pre-approved" actions optional. https://www.getmacha.com/blog/how-to-automate-refunds-in-zendesk (competitor blog, secondary).
- Estimates (no source, labelled): with food-delivery AOV about Rs 400-430 (A1, PrimeInvestor chart https://datawrapper.dwcdn.net/dTluu/3), an auto-approved voucher cap of Rs 100-150 limits worst-case exposure to about a third of one order. Lateness tiers suggestion: under 20 min over, apology only; 20-45 min over, voucher; over 45 min over, 50% refund of item value, capped. Fair-use idea: after N refund/voucher grants in a rolling 30 days (N is a tenant setting, suggest 3), the case is never auto-approved and the manager sees the history. Voucher-first for coffee (remake or voucher), refund second.
- Simulator anchors (A1): AOV Rs 250-500 for generic cloud kitchens (https://www.restaurantindia.in/article/cloud-kitchen-business-in-mumbai-costs-profit-margins-and-growth-opportunities.16738); about 30 min average delivery (company claim, https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-makers/heres-why-swiggy-doubles-down-on-10-minute-delivery-while-zomato-retreats-65673.htm); Swiggy 4.07 orders per user per month FY26 (https://inc42.com/buzz/over-35-of-swiggy-users-try-multiple-services-but-order-frequency-slips/). No sourced coffee ticket size, late rate, missing-item rate or repeat rate. Spec ratios check out as plausible: Spice Route about 2.0 orders per customer per month, BrewCo about 3.0; ticket rates 5% and 2.7% of orders are estimates.

### 2.5 What a pilot buyer needs to see (task item 4)

Synthesised from vendor-authored evaluation guides (weak individually, consistent together; the Indian ops-head view is inference):
- Test on the buyer's own cases, not canned prompts; sample messy cases; measure verified resolution, not deflection; audit at least 5% of AI-resolved cases. https://querypal.com/blog/how-to-evaluate-ai-support-agent-before-deployment ; https://www.lorikeetcx.ai/articles/head-of-ai-evaluate-ai-agents-customer-support-2026
- Hard gates: any unsupported policy claim, sensitive data exposure, unauthorized action, missed high-risk escalation, tool-call loop pauses the pilot. Define escalation triggers in writing; document who approves exceptions and with what evidence. https://www.kommunicate.io/blog/ai-customer-support-rfp-template/ ; https://www.usefini.com/guides/ai-customer-support-platforms-pilot-leads
- Complete audit log of messages, tool calls and decisions; data retention and training terms; rollback or fallback queue if the vendor fails; weekly review of escalations; topic-scoped pilot with holdout and fixed decision date. https://www.twig.so/blog/how-long-ai-support-pilot-before-evaluating-results
- Inference for a Spice Route / BrewCo ops head: (a) rupees at risk per day and the hard cap; (b) one screen showing why a refund is proposed (evidence, policy citation, checks) and a one-click approve/reject; (c) what the customer was told and when; (d) what happens at 11 pm on a Friday when the payment provider times out (the honest "we are checking" message, then reconciliation); (e) per-outlet numbers (BrewCo Baner); (f) cost per case versus a support agent's.

### 2.6 Risks (task item 5)

- R-a Hallucinated promises. Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024 BCCRT 149 (14 Feb 2024): tribunal held the airline liable for its chatbot's wrong refund-policy statement and rejected the argument that the chatbot is a separate entity; award figures differ between reports. https://manatt.com/insights/newsletters/advertising-law/ai-gone-wild-airline-has-to-honor-a-refund-policy ; https://dailyhive.com/canada/air-canada-chatbot-decision . Canadian tribunal, so persuasive not binding in India, but the same logic is likely to appeal to Indian consumer forums (inference). Fits hard rule 7: only tool-confirmed facts in customer messages.
- R-b Prompt injection. OWASP lists prompt injection as the top LLM risk; indirect injection (payload in content the model reads, such as retrieved policy text or an order note) is hardest to filter; the recurring advice is to limit authority (read-only tools, scoped refund tool, allow-listed arguments, human approval for irreversible actions), not to rely on classifiers. Secondary sources only: https://appsecsanta.com/ai-security-tools/prompt-injection-guide ; https://aicsr.georgelambert.org/output/references/articles/owasp-llm01.md . ResolveDesk already matches the advice (hard rules 4, 6, 9, business-rule ceiling c1cc356); the gap is demonstrating it in evals.
- R-c Refund abuse and AI-faked evidence: see F14. Photos should not be an auto-approval basis in v1 (no image handling in spec).
- R-d DPDP Act 2023 / Rules 2025. Rules notified 13 Nov 2025; phased: Data Protection Board immediately, consent-manager registration about Nov 2026, most obligations (notice, consent, security, breach reporting) about 12-13 May 2027 (sources give 12 or 13 May). Penalty ceiling Rs 250 crore for failing reasonable security safeguards; breach-notification failure Rs 200 crore per one explainer; 72-hour breach window was NOT confirmed in sources, verify. Processors act on the fiduciary's instructions, need contractual flow-down, sub-processors need authorisation, deletion when purpose ends; cross-border transfers allowed unless a government negative list says otherwise (commentary). Sources: https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fcentre-notifies-dpdp-rules-implementation-planned-in-phases-spread-over-12-18-months-3798465 ; https://www.bdo.in/getmedia/87552494-85d2-4edb-95da-0f118fdb3052/The-DPDP-Rules_BDO-India_2025.pdf ; https://dev.to/mr_manushukla/dpdp-compliance-costs-for-indian-startups-what-to-budget-before-13-may-2027 ; https://amlegals.com/data-fiduciary-processor-obligations-dpdpa/ ; https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/data-processor-duties-under-indias-dpdp-act-2023/ . Relevance: the tenant is the data fiduciary, ResolveDesk is a processor, LLM providers (Gemini, Groq) are sub-processors, and Gemini/Groq calls may move customer text abroad. v1 uses synthetic data only (PROJECT.md section 2), so there is no live exposure; the point is showing awareness: PII minimisation in prompts, retention, deletion, audit.

2. Is the 50% for more than 45 min late fixed, or may E3.2 introduce graduated tiers (R7)? It changes expected values in scenario sr-late-001.
3. Fair-use threshold (R4): is 3 grants in 30 days an acceptable default, and may it be a tenant setting? Accept R4 and R9 as new stories?
4. Auto-approve voucher cap: about Rs 150 as a vertical-pack default, or per tenant?
5. BrewCo order values: no sourced coffee ticket size; use the mid-market estimates in R12 or supply a real menu?
6. Legal items (E-Commerce Rules, RBI TAT, DPDP 72-hour breach window) rest on explainer summaries. Verify against the official texts before the README quotes them as law?
